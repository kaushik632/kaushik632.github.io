NHTSA Vehicle Defect Risk Prioritization Model

A severity- and detectability-weighted approach to automotive quality triage & product backlog optimization.

Executive Summary

This project built a normalized FMEA risk model across ~10,000 NHTSA safety complaints to surface the categories that volume-only ranking buries—proving that a data-driven severity lens reshuffles the entire priority queue.

Section 01 // The Business Problem

Data Pipeline Challenge & the Volume Trap

Automotive safety teams operate under fixed engineering budgets. When a recall decision lands on the table, the instinct is to prioritize by complaint volume—whichever component generates the most tickets gets attention first. This is fast, defensible, and wrong.

Volume conflates frequency with severity. A steering shimmy at highway speed generates hundreds of complaints; an airbag deployment failure generates far fewer—because the people involved in airbag failures are less likely to file a follow-up complaint. Volume-first triage systematically under-prioritizes the categories where failure consequences are most severe.

The core question: can a weighted risk model surface high-severity, low-volume defect categories that volume-only ranking buries—and does the re-ranking change the priority queue enough to matter?

The data engineering challenge was non-trivial. NHTSA complaint records list multiple components per complaint as a single comma-delimited string, and many category names themselves contain commas. A naive split fragments categories and inflates frequency counts.

The solution was a complaint-to-component junction table built with comma-aware parsing logic—correctly attributing each complaint to its component categories without double-counting or fragmenting multi-word category names. This junction table became the foundation for all downstream frequency, severity, and detectability calculations.

Section 02 // SQL Methodology & FMEA RPN Logic

Why Raw Multiplication Fails—And Why NTILE Fixes It

A standard FMEA Risk Priority Number multiplies Severity × Frequency × Detectability. But when those three dimensions live on different scales—severity scores from 1–5, frequency counts in the thousands, reporting lag in days—raw multiplication produces a score dominated by whichever dimension has the largest range.

The fix is decile normalization. SQL's NTILE(10) window function maps each dimension onto an equal 1–10 scale, preserving rank order within each dimension while eliminating scale bias. The resulting RPN score weights all three factors equally.

SQL // NTILE Normalization & RPN Scoring
-- Normalizing Severity, Frequency, and Reporting Lag via NTILE
WITH CategoryMetrics AS (
    SELECT
        component_category,
        COUNT(complaint_id) AS total_complaints,
        AVG(severity_score) AS avg_severity,
        AVG(days_to_report) AS avg_reporting_lag
    FROM nhtsa_junction_table
    GROUP BY component_category
)
SELECT
    component_category,
    NTILE(10) OVER (ORDER BY total_complaints) AS freq_rank,
    NTILE(10) OVER (ORDER BY avg_severity) AS sev_rank,
    NTILE(10) OVER (ORDER BY avg_reporting_lag) AS detect_rank,
    (NTILE(10) OVER (ORDER BY avg_severity) *
     NTILE(10) OVER (ORDER BY total_complaints) *
     NTILE(10) OVER (ORDER BY avg_reporting_lag)) AS RPN_Score
FROM CategoryMetrics;

This approach preserves the multiplicative interaction of FMEA—a category that scores high on all three dimensions compounds its risk—while ensuring that no single dimension dominates the output through scale alone.

Section 03 // Key Insights & Product Backlog

Steering vs Air Bags—And the 0.2% Anomaly

Under volume-only ranking, STEERING leads the priority queue. Under RPN scoring, STEERING drops out of the top tier entirely. AIR BAGS—with 13x higher severity rates despite lower complaint volume—takes the #1 position alongside STRUCTURE.

The most striking result was EQUIPMENT. At roughly 0.2% of total complaint volume, EQUIPMENT ranked dead last on every frequency chart. But its severity scores were among the highest in the dataset, and its average reporting lag exceeded 7 months—indicating low detectability. The RPN model surfaced EQUIPMENT into the Next tier, rescuing it from the bottom of a volume-only queue.

A category representing 0.2% of complaints surfaced into the priority tier—a finding invisible to any volume-based ranking system.

Tier Priority Categories
NOW Immediate Action Air Bags, Structure
NEXT Scheduled Sprint Seats, Vehicle Speed Control, Lane Departure, Equipment, Forward Collision Avoidance, Electrical System
LATER Backlog / Monitor Steering, Service Brakes, Tires, Wheels, Exterior Lighting, and remaining categories

This Now / Next / Later framework translates directly into a product backlog. Engineering teams can allocate investigation and recall resources to the Now tier first, schedule the Next tier into upcoming sprints, and monitor the Later tier for volume spikes that might trigger re-scoring.
